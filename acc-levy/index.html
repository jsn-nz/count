What the earners’ levy is

The ACC earners’ levy pays for injuries that happen outside work: at home, on the road or playing sport. Everyone who earns income from work pays it. For employees it is built into PAYE, so it does not usually appear as a separate line on a payslip.

For 2026–27 the levy is 1.75% of gross earnings, up to $156,641 a year. The most anyone pays is $2,741.22. The rate includes GST. It is set to rise again from 1 April 2027.

Other ACC levies

  • Work levy: paid by employers and the self-employed, based on industry risk. Employees do not pay it.
  • Working safer levy: a small flat levy paid alongside the work levy.
  • Motor vehicle levy: collected through vehicle licensing (rego) and petrol.

Self-employed people and contractors are invoiced by ACC directly; see contractor tax.

FAQs

What is the ACC earners' levy rate for 2026–27?

1.75% of gross earnings (GST inclusive), charged on income up to $156,641.

Is the ACC levy included in PAYE?

Yes. The PAYE your employer deducts is income tax plus the ACC earners’ levy.

What is the maximum ACC earners' levy?

$2,741.22 for 2026–27, which is 1.75% of the $156,641 cap.

Is ACC charged on redundancy pay?

No. Redundancy payments and retiring allowances are not liable for the earners’ levy.
