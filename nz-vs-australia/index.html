How the two systems differ

  • Tax-free threshold: Australia taxes nothing on the first A$18,200. New Zealand taxes from the first dollar at 10.5%.
  • Brackets: Australia’s rates are 15%, 30%, 37% and 45% (above A$190,000). New Zealand’s are 10.5%, 17.5%, 30%, 33% and 39% (above NZ$180,000).
  • Levies: Australia adds a 2% Medicare levy on all taxable income. New Zealand adds the 1.75% ACC earners’ levy, capped at $156,641 of earnings.
  • Retirement saving: Australian employers pay 12% superannuation on top of salary and nothing comes out of your pay. In New Zealand, KiwiSaver takes 3.5% from your pay and your employer adds 3.5%.
  • Tax year: Australia runs 1 July to 30 June and most people file a return. New Zealand runs 1 April to 31 March and most employees never file.

Moving across the Tasman

New Zealand citizens can live and work in Australia on a Special Category Visa and are normally treated as Australian tax residents once settled there, so the resident rates above apply. Pay is only half the comparison: rent, groceries, transport and the exchange rate decide how far it goes.

If you have a New Zealand student loan, it generally starts to attract interest once you have been overseas for six months, and repayments are set by your balance instead of your income.

FAQs

Do you pay more tax in NZ or Australia?

On the same number of dollars, income tax plus levies is 24.6% of a NZ$100,000 salary in New Zealand and 22.5% of an A$100,000 salary in Australia. Australia is lighter at low incomes because of its tax-free threshold, and heavier at the top, where its 45% rate exceeds New Zealand’s 39%.

What is $100,000 after tax in Australia compared with NZ?

A$100,000 leaves A$77,480 in Australia. NZ$100,000 leaves NZ$75,373 in New Zealand before KiwiSaver.

Is superannuation deducted from my pay in Australia?

No. The 12% super guarantee is paid by your employer on top of your salary. Check whether a quoted package is “plus super” or “including super”.

Do New Zealanders pay the Medicare levy in Australia?

Yes, if they are Australian tax residents. New Zealand citizens living in Australia are generally eligible for Medicare and pay the 2% levy.
