Secondary Tax in NZ: Codes and Calculator (2026–27)

Work out which secondary tax code applies to your second job, how much is deducted, and whether you are likely to get some back.

Secondary tax codes for 2026–27

Your second job is taxed at a single flat rate, chosen by your total yearly income from all sources (both jobs, plus any benefit or NZ Super). Your main job — the one that pays the most — keeps tax code M.

Is secondary tax higher?

No. There is no extra tax for having two jobs — your total income is taxed exactly as if one employer paid it all. Your main job has already used up the lower tax brackets, so the second job is taxed at the rate your top dollars fall into.

Example: $60,000 main job + $15,000 second job = $75,000. That falls in the SH band (30.0%), so the second job has $4,500.00 PAYE withheld. The tax actually due on that extra income is $4,500.00 — the same, because the whole second income sits in one bracket.

The flat rate is only exact when all your second-job income sits inside one bracket. If your total straddles a threshold you will be slightly over-taxed; if your income changes during the year it can go either way. Inland Revenue squares this up automatically after 31 March. If you are consistently over-taxed, you can apply for a tailored tax code so less is deducted each pay.

Student loans and second jobs

On a secondary code with SL (for example S SL), repayments are 12% of every dollar from the second job, because the $24,128 threshold is used by your main job. If your total income is below the threshold you can ask IRD for a special deduction rate.

FAQs

What is the secondary tax rate in NZ?

It depends on your total yearly income: SB is 10.5% ($0 – $15,600); S is 17.5% ($15,601 – $53,500); SH is 30.0% ($53,501 – $78,100); ST is 33.0% ($78,101 – $180,000); SA is 39.0% (Over $180,000). The 1.75% ACC earners’ levy is added on top.

Which job should be my main job?

The one that pays the most. It uses tax code M (or M SL with a student loan) and gets the lower tax brackets; every other job uses a secondary code.

Do I get secondary tax back?

Only the part that was over-withheld. Inland Revenue works out your tax on total income after 31 March and automatically refunds any excess or bills any shortfall.

What if my second job income changes during the year?

Tell your employer a new tax code if your expected total income moves into a different band. Using a code that is too low means a tax bill at the end of the year.
