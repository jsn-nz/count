A pay rise never leaves you worse off

New Zealand tax is progressive: moving into a higher bracket only changes the rate on the dollars above the threshold. The tax on everything you already earned stays the same, so more gross pay always means more take-home pay.

What can change is how much of each extra dollar you keep. Between $15,600 and $53,500 you keep about 81 cents in the dollar after PAYE and ACC; above $78,100 it drops to about 65 cents. If you receive Working for Families or other income-tested support, those payments can reduce as your income rises. That is separate from PAYE and not included here.

Asking for a rise? Know the number

FAQs

How much of a pay rise do I keep after tax in NZ?

After PAYE and the 1.75% ACC levy you keep between about 61% and 88% of each extra dollar, depending on your bracket. KiwiSaver and student loan deductions reduce that further.

Will a pay rise push me into a higher tax bracket and cost me money?

No. Only the income above the bracket threshold is taxed at the higher rate, so your take-home pay always goes up.

How much extra is a rise from $75,000 to $80,000?

$3,355.50 a year after PAYE and ACC, or $64.53 a week.

Does KiwiSaver come out of a pay rise?

Yes. Your contribution is a percentage of gross pay, so it rises with your salary, as does your employer’s contribution.
